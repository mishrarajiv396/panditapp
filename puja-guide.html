    Five adhyay

    The Satyanarayan Katha

    A faithful retelling for understanding and listening. At the altar, read the complete authorised katha from your printed book.

    Reader vs. ritual text. The five chapters below are a retelling, not a verbatim scripture. Published katha books differ in wording and detail. For the actual vrata, read from an authorised book or invite a pandit.

    Source status. Katha summaries are based on the traditional Satyanarayan Vrat Katha and are marked as retellings. They are awaiting pandit review. Regional versions may name characters differently or add local episodes.

    Tithi timing matters. Purnima or Ekadashi may span two evenings. The date shown is based on the tithi at sunrise. Many families observe the vrata on the evening when the tithi prevails at moonrise; follow your family panchang or pandit. Avoid Rahu Kaal for beginning the puja.

    Read before you begin

    Sources, review status & safety

    This guide is built to reduce confusion, not to replace the living tradition of a family pandit.

    What is sourced

    Every mantra card has a source note. The Ganesh, kalash, Satyanarayan, havan, purnahuti and kshama mantras are traditional and widely published. The katha chapters are retellings, not verbatim scripture.

    Review status: awaiting empanelled pandit review. Until then, treat pronunciation and sequence as a study aid.

    What varies by family

    The order of shodashopachar, the number of ahutis, the aarti, the katha edition, the offerings and the visarjan method all vary. Follow your sampradaya. A parent’s or family pandit’s instruction comes first.

    Purnima and Ekadashi dates can differ by a day between panchangs because of tithi timing and local observance.

    Privacy & data

    Names, gotra, checklist ticks and progress are stored in your browser’s localStorage. This page has no analytics, no account and no server sync. Clearing browser data removes everything.

    The print/save-as-PDF action is handled by your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

    Fire, water & safety

    Keep a water bucket and sand near the havan. Never leave a flame unattended. Do not use plastic, printed paper or synthetic flowers in the fire. Dispose of flowers and ash responsibly; do not put plastic into water during visarjan.

    If your home or building does not allow fire, ask a pandit about a safe alternative.
