Our story

One family’s pooja became an operating standard for thousands.

In 2019, a Griha Pravesh in Gurugram went wrong in the most ordinary way. The pandit was sincere, but the samagri was incomplete, the muhurat had been copied from a WhatsApp forward, and the society gate pass was never arranged. The family spent the morning apologising to guests instead of settling into their new home.

We rebuilt that day as a checklist. Verified pandits. A panchang engine that computes the muhurat for the exact city. A numbered samagri box packed in ritual order. A gate pass generated before the pandit leaves. And a price quoted once, with the dakshina already inside it.

Today the same standard runs across 1,057 Indian and NRI cities, districts and famous places, six regional traditions and more than 12,000 rituals - from a 30-minute desk blessing to a four-hour wedding-week havan.

Punya-Flow

The ritual ends. The responsibility does not.

Most families are left with a bin full of flowers, ash and prasad after the pandit leaves. In many societies that is a violation, and in every case it is a quiet disrespect to what was offered.

Punya-Flow is our closed loop: the pandit collects, we segregate and compost, and you receive a simple impact note. It is included in every all-inclusive booking, and available as a standalone add-on for self-performed poojas.

  • Collected by the same pandit, at the end of the ritual
  • Composted at a licensed Gurugram facility
  • Flowers upcycled into havan incense, not dumped in a landfill
  • Impact note shared on WhatsApp within seven days
